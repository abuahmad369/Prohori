01Executive summary

Vibe96 does one unusual thing well. It has a clear, named audit framework for AI-built software, with a human review on top. That is its moat and the reason to take it seriously. Almost everything around it is thin, early, or missing: discovery, deployment, customisation, buyer trust, builder economics, and the technical surface itself. Its own published numbers (68 apps, 24 selling builders, 2 customer deployments) show an operation still at launch scale.

The promotional explainer by Sumit Saha (Learn with Sumit, 17 October 2026) frames the problem as Trust, Distribution and Delivery. That framing is right. The product today ships the slogan with the thinnest version of each pillar: a one-time audit, a keyword search over about 68 listings, and a 48-working-hour human deployment onto a prepaid VPS. A successor can take this market by turning each pillar from a promise that people fulfil by hand into an automated system: a quality score that keeps updating, try-before-buy sandboxes, semantic discovery, serverless, container and VPS hosting tiers, and a durable AI agent that lives inside the buyer's project.

This dossier is the plan for building that successor. It is written for a founder with a team, or a serious plan to assemble one. It takes positions: it recommends specific technology, specific commercial terms, and a specific first ninety days.

02What Vibe96 actually is in October 2026

Positioning, legal entity, scale, team, and the actual product footprint — because the sales copy on the homepage is not what you'd compete with.

Positioning

Vibe96 sells itself as the first marketplace for vibe-coded software — apps built with Claude Code, OpenAI Codex, Cursor, Lovable, Bolt, and similar — with every listing audited by an automated system and reviewed by a person before it goes live. The pitch is end-to-end: buy the code, hire humans to change it, host it on Vibe96's VPS, keep upgrading with Nova (its in-platform AI developer). Buyers get a one-time single-site licence; builders keep 65% of each sale.

The licence page names Royex Technologies LLC, Dubai, United Arab Emirates as the marketplace operator, with the licence issued on each seller's behalf. The About page separately names Vibe96 FZ-LLC, which is the trading entity. Governing law for the licence is the DIFC — Dubai International Financial Centre. Technical fingerprint, inferred from the network trace

03Every surface, audited

A senior-reviewer pass, surface by surface. For each, what's actually there, what's good about it, and what's missing.

3.1 Buy software — the marketplace itself

What's there. Filterable catalogue with sensible facets (category, built-with tool, price band, tech), pagination, 24 per page, a 3-slot compare page, a product-detail template that is genuinely rich: features broken down by subsystem, technical specification, server requirements, deployment notes, release history, audit sub-scores on six axes, builder sidebar, similar-products shelf. Keyword search placeholder tries to lead buyers with "Real estate CRM" / "Website with CMS" style exemplars.

What's good. The product-detail page is the strongest content asset on the site. The 6-axis audit (secrets, deps, authz, DB access rules, structure, build reproducibility) is a legible, defensible rubric; the fact that each listing shows sub-scores plus a reviewer-notes line is better than CodeCanyon or Gumroad do.

What's missing.

  • Keyword search only, as far as I could see. No semantic retrieval, no "match me a project from this brief", no autocomplete, no saved searches, no alerts for new listings in a category.
  • No code preview. Buyers can see the demo and the screenshots but cannot read a single file. For a code marketplace, this is the biggest trust gap.
  • No try-before-buy sandbox. The demo is a shared public URL. There is nothing a buyer can touch, break, or fork.
  • No buyer reviews. Testimonials are from builders. Buyers never rate anything post-purchase.
  • No SBOM, no CVE refresh. The 94/100 score is from the day of audit (e.g. 6 Oct 2026). Nothing refreshes against vulnerability feeds. A listing with a Laravel pinned to a CVE'd minor version keeps its badge.
  • Compare limited to 3, datasheet-style only. No performance benchmarks, dependency counts, test coverage, codebase-size trajectories.
  • No freemium / open-core. Pure one-time licence, no trial, no community edition, no money-back; all sales final.
  • Slug fragility. /project/furnivo → 404 vs /project/furnivo-2 works. Projects re-ingested earn -2, -3, -5 suffixes. Both SEO-costly and user-confusing.

3.2 Get it Live — managed deployment

What's there. A clear five-step flow — pick project (or upload / connect Git), pick server, pick publishing, pick optional expert hours, pay — with a 48 working-hour SLA from "receipt of everything." Hosting is private KVM-virtualised VPS on Intel Xeon / AMD EPYC, in ISO 27001 / SOC 2 Type II facilities, Linux or fully-licensed Windows Server, 1 Gbps dedicated IP, hardware RAID 10. $8.58–$161/mo on 12-month term. Daily off-server backups kept 7 days. Mobile publishing is $399 per submission pass. 99.9% uptime guarantee is carried from the data-centre operator (not from Vibe96 itself).

What's good. The build-up (private VPS, named engineers, 2-hour critical response, 72-hour security-incident disclosure, final backup downloadable for 3 months after hosting ends) is well-articulated and reads like someone who has run production. Pricing is in the right band — competitive with Hetzner Cloud + managed support.

What's missing.

  • VPS-only. No serverless, no edge, no container PaaS, no scale-to-zero. For AI-built Next.js apps this is actively worse than the buyer deploying straight to Vercel themselves.
  • 7-day backup / no PITR. Neon, Supabase, RDS all offer point-in-time recovery to the second. Daily snapshot with 7-day retention is below modern.
  • Human SLA. 48 working hours is a human operation. No automation, no credit on breach, no public status page, no incident archive.
  • No preview deployments per release. A new builder version arrives, there is no preview URL, no diff, no canary.
  • Monthly minimum is high for small buyers of $29–$79 apps. Buying a $40 app and spending $240+ to host it for a year inverts the value stack.
  • Lock-in posture. Buyers can download files and request a full copy, but there is no documented one-click migration path to another host.

3.3 Hire experts — the labour layer

What's there. Two paths. The /experts page sells hourly packs (consultant $200/hr, senior dev $100, dev $80, DevOps $75, designer $70, mobile publisher $70; 2–5 hour minimums; $150–$1,950 packs). The /developers page runs a fixed-price scoped-work funnel: free discovery call, Vibe96 introduces one or two vetted builders who already score 85+ on the audit, they quote a fixed price, buyer pays Vibe96 which holds funds until delivery is accepted, delivery is re-audited.

What's good. The /developers fixed-price path is better than many freelance platforms: there is real escrow and the hand-off goes through the same audit as any listing. That's a genuinely good control.

What's missing.

  • No expert profiles. You pay first, then "we match you." A 2010 pattern. No portfolios, no verified past work, no response-time stats, no ratings, no time-zone.
  • No scheduling preview. No calendar, no bookable slot.
  • Hourly path has no escrow, only the scoped path does.
  • Nova doesn't auto-escalate. When Vibe Studio hits something complex, it should draft a brief, estimate hours, and route to a shortlist. Today, there is no such bridge.

3.4 Sell & earn — the builder flow

What's there. A seven-step submission flow (code + demo → automated audit → fix if under 75/100 → listing → media → seller agreement → human review). 65/35 split. No listing fee. $10 welcome + $50 first-live bonus. Monthly payouts on the 5th, $200 minimum, Wise/Payoneer/bank. Patch releases run a lighter admin approval; new versions run the full audit again. Builders must support buyers 30 days and provide updates for 12 months.

What's good. The audit rubric is the backbone and it is clearly written. The commercial terms are competitive with CodeCanyon's new 50% cut.

What's missing.

  • Audits are point-in-time. No continuous re-score when deps age or CVEs land. A listing's badge drifts away from reality the day after it's issued.
  • $200 monthly minimum is slow for small builders. Stripe Connect Express can pay weekly with no minimum in supported countries.
  • No builder analytics. Only a sales list. No impression → compare → add-to-bag → paid funnel, no listing-copy A/B, no price-elasticity signal.
  • No module / plugin sub-marketplace. A builder cannot sell a reusable Stripe adapter or audit-pass auth kit for other builders to drop into their templates.
  • No GitHub-triggered re-audit. Every push should re-run the audit and update the live score.
  • No LLM-generated changelogs. Release notes are hand-written by builders.

3.5 Vibe Studio — the AI customisation layer

What's there (beta). Nova as a coordinator; Claude generates code, ChatGPT generates images. The flow is Discuss → Plan (checklist points with per-task estimates) → Design (preview on the real page, with device variants) → Develop. The live site keeps running until the new version passes checks; rollback is available for 7 days. Pay-as-you-go, $5 free credit, $10 top-ups. Kimi, DeepSeek listed as planned additions.

What's good. The design-preview-before-code flow is a strong UX idea. Showing a cost estimate per task and the real cost after is better than most IDE-side agents.

What's missing.

  • No stated durability. The page says nothing about tasks surviving tab closes, server restarts, or multi-hour runs. That may exist inside the beta, but it is not promised.
  • Hard-coded provider pair. No model router, no AI Gateway, no BYOK, no per-task budget cap, no transparent fallback.
  • No human handoff. No seamless Nova → expert ticket with pre-filled brief and estimate.
  • No eval harness. How does Vibe96 know Nova got better this week? No published eval set.

3.6 Trust and security surface

Hosting security page is substantive — isolated KVM, layered firewall, SSL auto-renew, 24/7 datacentre ops behind Mon-Fri 9-18 Dubai-time Vibe96 ops, 72-hour security-incident disclosure commitment, GDPR-aligned handling, DPA on request. The gap is the public evidence of real operation: no status page, no post-mortems, no SLA percentage track record, no transparent incident log, no SOC 2 of Vibe96 itself (the SOC 2 cited is the data centre's, inherited). For a buyer in financial services or healthcare, that gap is the deal.

3.7 Content, SEO, and brand

Sitemap exposes a /blog with 16 articles (all authored by "Marketing Team"), seven guides covering deploy-Lovable, deploy-Bolt, publish-to-app-store, fix-vibe-coded-app, security-checklist, buy, sell. The blog is young but on-strategy. The homepage carries a short, well-produced marketing site, well-written in a quiet "editorial trust" voice. English only across the whole site; USD only; VAT only for UAE.

04The competitive landscape

Where Vibe96 sits inside a crowded, three-category market, and what the incumbents and challengers actually do.

Three categories you compete against

  1. Classic code marketplaces. CodeCanyon (Envato, acquired by Shutterstock in 2024), Codester, Gumroad for code, independent script shops. A developer blog reports that Envato raised its cut to 50% from July 2026 and dropped exclusivity; check Envato's author terms before relying on it. One widely shared essay argues CodeCanyon sales have fallen sharply since 2018. Catalogue is JS/PHP/ASP.NET/Java heavy. No live audit, no managed hosting, no AI customisation.
  2. Finished-business marketplaces. Flippa (volume, 5–10% success fee, built-in escrow), Acquire.com formerly MicroAcquire (SaaS over ~$250K ARR, closes 10–20% below listing), Empire Flippers (curated), ExitBid (5-day auctions, $199 flat listing, 0% commission, ~66% rejection). These sell running businesses, not source code.
  3. New vibe-coded-app marketplaces. Direct competitors to Vibe96: Vibe Coded / vibecodedshop, VibeCoding.Market (launched June 2025 for "abandoned and failed projects"), Unloved. All early, none with Vibe96's audit depth or Dubai-based operations backing them.

And the AI-builder platforms that could swallow this wedge

The real strategic risk is not CodeCanyon, which is in decline. The real risk is that the AI-builder platforms where these apps are made add distribution themselves:

  • Lovable. $330M raise December 2025 at $6.6B. Exports React, Supabase, Stripe. If they add a marketplace, they own the funnel.
  • Base44. Bought by Wix June 2025. Code export is limited today; a template exchange is the obvious next move.
  • Replit. Agent + Templates already exist. Full git access, 50+ languages. A sell-your-template button is a quarter of work away.
  • v0 (Vercel), Bolt (StackBlitz), Claude Code. Each has native distribution — Vercel Deploy, StackBlitz hosting, GitHub integration — that competes directly with Get it Live without charging extra.

And the audit-tool specialists

VibeChex publishes an AI-app audit methodology directly competing with Vibe96's rubric. Mend AIBoM, sbomqs, sbomaudit, and AI Code Validator all offer SBOM scoring and AI-code CVE scanning as standalone tools or SaaS. None has a marketplace attached — which is Vibe96's advantage — but any of them could ship one.

06The thesis for a successor

Vibe96 is a catalogue plus managed services. The successor should be a programmable code-commerce platform — buying a project is the first API call of an automated supply chain: fork, provision, deploy, instrument, iterate, upgrade.

Everything that is a human-operated promise today becomes an automated primitive in the successor. The marketplace UI is one of several clients onto those primitives — the others are a CLI, an SDK, webhooks, and an MCP server that agentic IDEs (Cursor, Claude Code, Codex, v0) can call directly. That shift is the moat: once discovery, licensing, and deployment are callable from inside the tool the buyer already lives in, the marketplace is the default distribution of AI-built software and competitors have to catch three primitives at once.

Four principles sit under this.

Principle 1 — Trust is a stream, not a stamp

A score issued once and shown forever is a stamp. A score that re-runs nightly against the CVE feed, re-generates the SBOM on every push, demotes the badge when a dep ages past safe, and emails the builder to fix it, is a stream. The successor's quality score is a time series the buyer can see go up and down. The audit becomes a living product, not a certification.

Principle 2 — Buyers must be able to touch the thing

Try-before-buy sandbox on every listing: a one-click, 24-hour, scoped-to-the-SKU private deployment of the real source with seed data. Buyers click around, break things, decide. Then a 7-day refund under $200 and a 30-day store-credit refund above closes the risk conversation. This is the single highest-conversion lever in the plan.

Principle 3 — Delivery is an escalator, not a stair

Three deployment tiers, chosen at checkout, that match the buyer's maturity: zero-ops serverless/edge (scale-to-zero, usage-priced), managed containers (for stateful stacks), private VPS (for the compliance-first buyer who wants dedicated metal). Preview deployments per release on every tier. One-click migrate between tiers as the buyer grows.

Principle 4 — The AI studio is a durable workspace, not a chat widget

Nova is rebuilt as an eve-based durable agent (Vercel's filesystem-first agent framework) per buyer project: a real filesystem, tools, skills, schedules, evals, and a git-backed state that survives tab closes, model failures, and multi-hour tasks. Model routing through Vercel AI Gateway: Claude for code, GPT for images, Gemini for long-context, with transparent fallback and per-task spend caps. Human-in-the-loop expert escalation is a Nova skill, not a separate web form.

07Goals, in SMART form

Six twelve-month targets. Specific, measurable, each tied to a KPI from §12.

  1. Catalogue depth. 500 live audited listings in the first 12 months (vs Vibe96's current 68), with ≥40% submitted directly from a connected GitHub repository that triggers a continuous audit.
  2. Buyer conversion. Reach 2.5% view-to-paid conversion on listings with a sandbox button. Measure your own baseline in Phase 1, before the sandbox ships, so the lift is provable.
  3. Trust re-score latency. Every live listing re-scored at most 24 hours after a new CVE lands in NVD/OSV against any pinned dependency.
  4. Deployment velocity. p50 time-from-paid-to-live under 15 minutes on serverless tier; p95 under 2 hours on container tier; VPS stays at 48 working-hour human SLA with auto-credit on breach.
  5. Builder payout cadence. Weekly Stripe Connect payouts with no minimum in every country Stripe Connect supports, plus Wise or Payoneer for builders elsewhere (Bangladesh and Pakistan, for example, are a large share of this builder audience). Earnings visible in a real-time dashboard.
  6. Platform margin. Reach $120K monthly gross profit by month 12 across marketplace commission, hosting margin, AI studio margin, and expert escrow fee. Mix: ~40% marketplace, ~35% hosting, ~15% AI studio, ~10% experts.

08Reference architecture

Concrete Vercel-native stack. Chosen because this is the one platform that lets a two-person team ship marketplace + sandbox + AI studio + deploy pipeline without re-inventing the primitives.

Frontend and edge

  • Next.js 16 App Router with Partial Prerendering (PPR) and Cache Components on Vercel. Marketplace shell static; cart, account, live quality score stream in.
  • Routing Middleware for geolocation (currency selection), A/B via Flags SDK, auth gating, bot-detection pre-route (Vercel BotID).
  • shadcn + Tailwind, dark mode default, 15+ locale next-intl, next/image and next/font.

Compute

  • Vercel Functions on Fluid Compute, Node 24 LTS, up to 5 GB bundle. Handles API, image processing, Playwright-driven demo-health checks, Python ML for embedding generation.
  • Vercel Sandbox for try-before-buy environments. Spin a real build of the listed source with seed data, scoped to a SKU, 24-hour TTL.
  • Vercel WebSockets for Nova streaming sessions and collaborative Vibe Studio edits.

Durable workflows

  • Workflow 5 WorkflowAgent (from @ai-sdk/workflow) for every long-running pipeline — audit → review → publish, purchase → provision → DNS → SSL → smoke test → handover, release → diff → canary → promote → notify. Deterministic retries, time-travel debug.
  • Vercel Queues for at-least-once events: listing.published, order.paid, deployment.ready, audit.cve_detected, payout.scheduled.
  • Vercel Cron for weekly re-scores, nightly CVE sweeps, demo-URL pings, Monday payout runs.

Data

  • Neon Postgres via Vercel Marketplace, with branch-per-preview and point-in-time recovery to the minute. Replaces nightly VPS backup outright.
  • pgvector on the same cluster for embeddings of README + architecture + feature list of every listing. Backs hybrid semantic + lexical search.
  • Vercel Blob (private) for source archives and release artifacts; signed with Vercel KMS.
  • Global Config (formerly Edge Config) for featured-shelf IDs, kill switches, global banner.

AI stack

  • AI SDK 7 + Vercel AI Gateway fronts every LLM call. Model routing by task shape, BYOK for enterprise buyers, zero-retention mode, per-user spend caps.
  • eve for Nova: durable session per project, filesystem, tools (git, test runner, deploy, image gen), skills (restyle, add-page, add-integration), subagents, schedules, evals.
  • Published eval set: 50 real customisation tasks, re-run weekly, score shown on the Studio page.

Commerce and payouts

  • Stripe Checkout + Connect Express + Stripe Tax — multi-currency, 100+ VAT/GST/Sales-Tax jurisdictions, weekly payouts.
  • Or Lemon Squeezy as Merchant of Record for the first six months while Stripe Tax is tuned.
  • Stripe Billing for hosting instalments.

Trust / audit engine

  • Audit pipeline runs as a Workflow 5 agent against every new push to a connected builder repo. Produces: 6-axis Vibe96-style score, SBOM in SPDX JSON, CVE list from OSV, license-compat (SPDX scanning), test-coverage summary (if lcov present), code-complexity signal (ESLint complexity, PHPStan level, Roslyn analyzers).
  • Nightly re-score cron hits every live listing against the current OSV feed; demotes the badge and emails the builder on regression.
  • Demo-URL health ping every 5 min — status rolls into the badge.

13Risks and open questions

Strategic risks

  • Builder adds marketplace first. Lovable or Replit launches a template exchange and keeps all the value. Mitigation: ship MCP + GitHub App early so the distribution flows through you no matter which builder made the code.
  • Vibe96 wakes up. They have 68 listings' worth of head start on supply and a documented audit rubric. Mitigation: a public, time-series quality score is a feature they would have to retrofit, which is slower than you shipping it fresh.
  • Trust failure. One listing with a credential-stealing payload reaches a buyer and the story kills the brand. Mitigation: audit worker runs in a hardened sandbox; code preview is read-only hash-sampled; sandbox demo runs scoped-to-SKU with no outbound egress.

Operational risks

  • Human-review bottleneck. Scaling listing review beyond 20/week is hard. Mitigation: automated audit covers 85% of decisions; human review becomes an exception path.
  • LLM cost blow-up. Nova running unbounded cooks a buyer's balance. Mitigation: hard per-task caps, per-user monthly caps, estimate before start, show real cost after.

Legal risks

  • Open-source licence contamination. A listing's audit misses GPL-covered code; buyer ships commercial on top. Mitigation: SPDX scanning is a required audit gate.
  • VAT / tax across 100 jurisdictions. Mitigation: Lemon Squeezy MoR for the first six months reduces this to zero.
  • Hosting liability. A buyer's app loses data on your tier. Mitigation: clear DPA, PITR on Neon, Blob versioning, documented RPO/RTO per tier.

14What to do this week

Six concrete steps that establish momentum before any funding conversation.

  1. Register the trading name and domain. Buy two or three candidates. If serious about founding, incorporate in a jurisdiction that gives optionality (Delaware C-Corp if US-raise intended; UK Ltd + DIFC if Gulf-focused).
  2. Scaffold the Next.js 16 + Vercel + Neon skeleton in the directory you already opened for this. One day's work. Push to GitHub, deploy to Vercel preview, so you have a URL to show.
  3. Write the audit rubric. Vibe96's six axes are a sensible baseline. Add two: license-compat (SPDX scan) and performance-budget (Lighthouse LCP ≤ 2.5s on seed demo).
  4. Prototype the sandbox. One Vercel Sandbox call that boots a known Next.js repo with seed data and returns a 24-hour URL. This is the demo you show to builder prospects.
  5. Pick 20 builder prospects. Look in the comment sections of vibe-coding videos, among builders already selling on CodeCanyon and Gumroad, and in the Lovable and Replit showcases. Reach out with a $100 first-listing bonus and a 70% share. Ten yeses are enough to seed the catalogue.
  6. Draft the one-pager that will become the fundraising deck. Thesis + market + architecture + roadmap + team + ask. The dossier you are reading is 80% of its content already.

Final read. Vibe96's own live figure of 2 apps deployed for customers sums up the opportunity. The pitch is good and the execution is still open. Building this is a bet that you can ship the automated pieces in twelve months faster than an incumbent can retrofit them, and that AI-coding tools will reward whoever ships the API-first version first.
